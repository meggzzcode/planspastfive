# Risks, stated plainly

1. **Amazon Associates final approval is pending, the 180-day window is running, and the account is not transferable.** The account that holds the tag (planspastfive-20) sits inside the owner's master Associates account, which also holds another site's tag. Its status could not be read as "approved"; the earlier check found no approval label, 0 sales and a banner asking the primary holder to finish payment details. Amazon's Operating Agreement says the agreement may not be assigned without Amazon's written approval, so **a buyer opens their own account and replaces the tag**. Amazon's rules do not require waiting for final approval: links may be used after applying, and three qualifying sales within 180 days of applying trigger the final review (`docs/AMAZON-ASSOCIATES.md`). Tracking is ON in `src/content/settings.json` (owner decision 2026-11-23) in `main` and `preview`; the production site only gets tagged links when a `[deploy]` commit is made. Until the site is public and indexed, tagged links in a private preview earn nothing, and the owner's own purchases never qualify. If no qualifying sales arrive in 180 days the application can be withdrawn and cannot be reinstated.
2. **Required disclosure wording** ("As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.") was read by Cowork in the Operating Agreement (updated Oct 15, 2025) on 2026-11-19 and is used exactly. While tracking is off, the footer and the note before the first product link say instead that links are ordinary and earn nothing yet; the required sentence is added once tracking is on. Owner to confirm that is acceptable.
3. **No Amazon pictures.** Amazon's rules bar storing or re-hosting its product images, and the approved routes (an Associates image tool, or the product API) are not available until the Creators API is approved (owner decision: no product photos until then). Product cards are text only; a picture slot exists for pictures you own. The check refuses any Amazon image address.
4. **Image rights.** Of 16 pictures, 12 are AI-generated (Canva, Lovable) and 4 are Pexels photographs. Each is `approved` in `src/content/images.json` on the owner's reading of the terms (2026-11-20), but none of the terms clearly says the images pass to a buyer, so transfer is recorded as `unverified`, and AI images carry no exclusivity or registrable copyright. A buyer should re-read the terms or replace pictures (`docs/IMAGE-RECORDS.md`). Photos of real products or people are not used.
5. **The CMS flow is unverified.** `.pages.yml` uses only documented keys but has never been opened in Pages CMS. Unknowns: the rich-text editor and curly-bracket markers, what the image field stores, the Publish buttons, branch switching. The fallback is editing files on GitHub.
6. **Deploy credits are thin.** 135 credits (about 9 deploys) until 2026-12-16. If credits run out the site pauses. Whether skipped builds, previews and branch deploys cost credits is unknown. Whether "Trigger deploy" honours the `[deploy]` rule is unknown.
7. **Product evidence is dated.** Twelve products are verified (Cowork, Nov 2026) and shown; stock, sellers and variants change, and a few details (Tim Holtz SKU, Sapphire contents, Staedtler and Scotch alternatives) await one more Amazon check. The mahjong large-print card is the 2026 edition and must be replaced for 2027. The monthly checklist covers re-checking.
8. **Trademark not cleared.** The name "Plans Past Five" has not been searched for conflicts (`docs/NAME-CHECK-planspastfive.md` in the earlier project holds the name notes only).
9. **Pre-revenue.** No traffic, no sales, no proven earnings. Five articles, a 12-product shop and a working publishing workflow are a starter asset, not a business. The asking price is an objective, not a valuation.
10. **Legal pages are plain-English drafts**, not legal advice. No publisher is named. The "How we use AI" paragraph repeats the earlier site's wording; the owner should confirm it is accurate.
11. **Pinned dependencies.** `package.json` pins exact versions (Astro 7.4.7, @astrojs/sitemap 3.7.4, @astrojs/markdown-remark 7.3.6, each at least two weeks old when chosen) and `package-lock.json` is committed; `npm audit` reports 0 vulnerabilities. To upgrade: run `npm install <package>@<exact version> --save-exact`, then `npm audit`, `npm run check` and `npm test`; commit only if all pass. `@astrojs/markdown-remark` is needed because the article product markers use a custom remark plugin (`plugins/remark-product.mjs`) that Astro 7's default Markdown processor does not run. Newer releases (for example Astro 7.4.13) were not used because they were under two weeks old when the upgrade was made.
