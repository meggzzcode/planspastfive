## Research Brief

**Working title:** Paint by Numbers for Adults: How to Choose a Kit You'll Actually Want to Display

**Reader intent:** Commercial and informational mix. Likely queries: "paint by numbers for adults", "best paint by numbers kits", "paint by numbers tips for beginners", "how long does paint by numbers take", "paint by numbers with frame and easel", "do you need to varnish paint by numbers", "paint by numbers light or dark first", "what comes in a paint by numbers kit". Long-tail questions seen in search results and top guides: how long it takes; do you paint light or dark first; how to stop numbers showing through; how to fix a wrong colour; how many colours for a beginner; do you need to varnish; how to store paint between sessions; rolled vs stretched canvas. Unanswered or thin in the top (mostly retailer) guides: how to judge a kit before buying (most guides are tips for painting, not for choosing), honest "where kits disappoint", and a plain "it varies" on time instead of a sales-friendly figure.

**Emotional hook:** The calm evening that ends in something she is proud to put on a shelf and can say "I made it" about. The fear underneath: buying a kit with tiny, unreadable areas and ending with a half-done canvas in a drawer.

**Template choice and reason:** shopping-edit, adapted. The reader is choosing a kit, so the order is finished result, what to look for, two picks, then mistakes, what to skip, where kits disappoint, finishing. No separate "starter basket" because the kit is the basket.

**Verified activity facts and sources** (numbers refer to Sources):
- Kit = pre-printed picture split into numbered areas, matching numbered paints; paint-by-number "kits include pre-printed board, paint pots, brush" (S5, S8).
- Origin: Detroit commercial artist Dan Robbins at Palmer Paint, kits launched early 1950s; recalled in memoir the Leonardo numbered-sections idea; critics questioned whether it was art (S8; date cross-checked in search snippets of NPR, Smithsonian listings, not read in full).
- Acrylic is the standard paint; dries quickly, keep pots closed (S3, S6).
- Dip only the brush tip; thin thick paint a drop at a time (S3, S4, S6).
- Numbers showing through light colours: dry, then second thin coat (S3, S4, S6).
- Start at top and work down/away from painting hand; let sections dry before painting next to them (S3, S4, S5).
- Photograph numbered canvas first (S3 and search snippets).
- Wrong colour: dry then paint over (S3, S4, S6).
- Thin layer touch-dry about 10 to 20 minutes, at least an hour before second coat or adjacent paint (S3, a kit maker's guidance).
- Order disagreement: S3 says dark first (covers numbers better); S4 gives light-to-dark numerical order or largest-to-smallest; S5 says smallest areas first; S6 says paint smallest sections first while hand is steady. Article says follow kit instructions.
- Beginner colour count: one guide suggests avoiding more than 30 colours for a first attempt (search-result summary of a retailer guide; see Open uncertainties).
- Reviewer complaints in S3: tiny sections, hard-to-read numbers (30s look like 20s), duplicate numbers in adjacent areas, running out of colours, missing frames or instructions, colours not matching the picture, photo cropped. These are one retailer's own reviews.
- Magnifier and toothpick tip for tiny areas (S5, S6).
- Rolled canvas needs flattening; pre-stretched reduces warping (S3, S6).
- Varnish optional, protects against dust/scratches, evens sheen; wait until paint fully dry (Liquitex: up to 2 weeks), at least 24 hours between coats; W&N: thin acrylic at least 24 hours before varnish, heavy layers up to a week; 1 to 3 thin coats (S1, S2).
- Faber-Castell Museum Series kits: six acrylic paints, one brush, chipboard easel, number guide, hang tab, illustrated instructions, online tutorial (layering, blending, detailing), ages 8+ (S9, Starry Night and Sunflowers pages, not the Monet kit). Water Lilies: 6 x 8 in printed canvas, six 5 ml acrylics, #2 brush, pop-up easel, pre-printed brush strokes, instructions teach wet-on-wet and washes (S10).

**Beginner requirements:** one kit; a flat, well-lit table; water cup and paper towel; phone to photograph the canvas; patience over several sittings. Nothing else is essential. Optional: magnifier, toothpick, varnish.

**Obstacles:** tiny unreadable areas, number show-through, smudging, dried paint, running out of paint, kits missing components, silent time commitment, fear of "not real art".

**Differentiation from existing articles:** air-dry clay and junk journal guides are make-it-from-scratch starter guides; this one is a buying guide for a guided activity. The hobbies hub only mentions paint nights in one card and points to the Cozy bundle; this article is the deeper guide behind that link.
