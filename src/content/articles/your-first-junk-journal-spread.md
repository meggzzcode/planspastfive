It is 5:40 on a Tuesday. The laptop is shut, the kettle is on, and somewhere in the kitchen there is a drawer you have been politely ignoring. It holds a cinema ticket, a postcard from a friend, the label off a genuinely excellent jam, and four takeaway menus. Congratulations. That drawer is your first spread.

A junk journal is a notebook you fill with small paper things, layered and stuck down however you like. Nobody marks it. Here are three ways to arrange a first page, so you are never staring at a blank one.

## Sort the drawer into piles

A dozen pieces is plenty. More than that and you are archiving, not journaling.

- **Things with writing:** tickets, receipts, a handwritten recipe card.
- **Things with a picture:** stamps, magazine corners, the second copy of a photo.
- **Things with texture:** a paper bag, tissue, a pressed flower.
- **One thing in a colour you love.** It becomes the thread through the page.

> The page does not need to be good. It needs to be yours, and finished before the tea goes cold.

## Three layouts

**The Big Hero.** One large piece (an envelope, a map, a page of a book) sits slightly off centre and takes up about half the spread. Tuck two small things under its edge and add a single caption. Best when you own one thing you adore.

**The Postcard Stack.** Three or four medium pieces, overlapped like a hand of cards, each showing a corner of the one beneath. Best for tickets, cards and photographs, because every piece gets a moment.

**The Row of Small Things.** A loose line of tiny pieces (stamps, labels, tea-bag tags) running across the lower third of the page, with plenty of space above. Best when nothing you own is big, and the quietest of the three.

Whichever you pick, arrange everything on the open pages before you stick anything down. Step back, squint, move one piece, and stop when you smile. Take a quick photo of the layout, so you can rebuild it after the cat walks across it.

## Stick it down, flat

Wet glue wrinkles thin paper, and a wrinkled ticket looks like it has had a long week. A dry adhesive runner keeps things crisp. The {{product:tombow-mono-permanent}} is our pick for the job, and you can work from the bottom layer up, pressing each piece down with your thumb for a few seconds.

## Finish with one line, in pen

Write a single line: a date, a place, a small joy. "Tuesday. Jam on toast. Better things are coming." Leave the rest of the white space alone, because a page that breathes looks more finished than one filled to the edges.

Leave the book open on the side table overnight so anything curling has time to calm down. Then look at the page on Thursday and see what the next one wants.
