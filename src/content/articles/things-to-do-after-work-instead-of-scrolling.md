You get home, put the keys in the bowl, sit down for one minute and pick up your phone. Forty minutes later the light has changed, you are not sure what you read, and dinner is still an idea. Nothing terrible happened. It is just not what you wanted from the evening.

Most lists of things to do instead are lists of hobbies, and a hobby asks for a decision at the exact moment you have none left. (We have one of those too: [twelve hobbies sorted by energy, budget and company](/journal/hobbies-for-busy-women/).) This list works differently. It sorts twelve small plans by **how much time you have**, and most of them need nothing you do not already own. Each says what it feels like, what it needs and the first step.

## Why the switch is hard, and what helps

Reaching for the phone takes no effort and no decision. Anything better has to be nearly as easy, so the work is mostly done before you sit down.

- **Move the phone, not your willpower.** Common Sense Media suggests charging your phone outside the bedroom and using an old-fashioned alarm clock, and the Sleep Foundation suggests a spot outside the bedroom where devices can park overnight. The same logic works at 6 p.m.: put it on a charger in the hallway or kitchen, and the sofa stops being a phone spot.
- **Make the phone slightly harder to use.** Common Sense Media also suggests keeping only everyday tools on the first screen and typing an app's name rather than tapping its icon, so there is a pause before you open it.
- **Decide earlier.** Planning guidance based on "if-then" plans says that spelling out the when, where and how ahead of time makes acting more likely. The research behind it comes with a caveat: it works best when you actually want the goal. So choose something you are curious about, in the morning or at lunch: "If I get home before 6, then I will make tea and sit by the window."
- **Set it out.** A notebook on the cushion, a mug on the counter, the stamp in the dish. Leave the next step visible.
- **Keep a default.** One plan from the 10-minute list that you do on any night you cannot choose. Nobody argues with a default.

You will still scroll sometimes. A plan that survives an off night is the whole idea.

## If you have 10 minutes

### 1. Tea in the window seat

**Feels like:** a full stop at the end of the workday.
**Needs:** a kettle, a mug and whichever seat has a view, even of a parking lot.
**First step:** make the tea before you take your coat off, and carry it to the seat without picking anything else up. Drink it looking out. When the mug is empty, you are done.

### 2. A walk that starts at the doorstep

**Feels like:** shaking out the day before it settles into the sofa.
**Needs:** shoes you can slip on. No route, no steps goal, no playlist.
**First step:** go once around the block, turning left at every corner. Notice one thing you have walked past a hundred times: a doorway, a tree, a hand-painted sign.

### 3. A handwritten note

**Feels like:** small, a bit old-fashioned, and surprisingly warm to send.
**Needs:** a card or a sheet of paper, a pen and a stamp, or a photo of it for texting.
**First step:** write three lines to someone who helped you recently. Say what they did and that you noticed. Put it by the door so it leaves the house with you in the morning.

### 4. A library card, in your pocket

**Feels like:** a quiet little door that has been unlocked for a while.
**Needs:** a few minutes and, usually, a photo ID with proof of your address.
**First step:** search your library's name plus "get a library card." Requirements differ by system, but those we read for this article asked adults for a photo ID and proof of address for a full card, and several offered an online card for ebooks and other digital resources. Boston Public Library, for instance, says its eCard takes about five minutes to sign up for and covers its online collection, with an in-person upgrade for physical items. Once you have it, reserve one book and let it be the thing waiting for you on Thursday.

## If you have 30 minutes

### 5. Tidy one shelf into a pretty one

**Feels like:** a tiny renovation with a finished result before the kettle cools.
**Needs:** one shelf, a damp cloth and things you already own.
**First step:** take everything off one shelf, wipe it, and put back only what you like looking at. Lean one book, stand one candle, add one small plant or a favorite postcard. Stop at one shelf. The rest of the room can wait.

### 6. A call, with a time on it

**Feels like:** better than texting, and shorter than you feared.
**Needs:** a friend, and a plan for the walk or the dishes you will do while you talk.
**First step:** send "Can I call you at 6:30? Twenty minutes, tops" in the morning. A set time and a set length make it easy to say yes, and easy to end.

### 7. A bath with a plan

**Feels like:** a small hotel stay with no luggage.
**Needs:** a tub, a towel and a plan for the phone, which is that it stays in the next room.
**First step:** decide the extras before you run the water: a paperback balanced on a tray, a podcast on a speaker across the room or just the sound of the tap. If you have a shower only, a hot shower, warm socks and ten pages of a book afterward has the same shape.

### 8. Make dinner slower

**Feels like:** cooking as the activity, not the chore between you and the evening.
**Needs:** something you already know how to cook, and a song or a radio station.
**First step:** pick one step to do properly that you usually rush: chop the onion into even pieces, toast the spices, set the table with a real napkin. Eat sitting down, with your phone out of reach.

## If you have a whole evening

### 9. One junk journal page, from your bag

**Feels like:** turning the evening's loose ends into something you can keep.
**Needs:** an old notebook, a glue stick, scissors and whatever is in your bag or kitchen drawer. About 45 minutes.
**First step:** pull out a ticket, a receipt and a tea-bag tag and arrange them on the page before sticking anything down. Our [first spread guide](/journal/your-first-junk-journal-spread/) has three layouts to copy, and the [supplies guide](/journal/junk-journal-supplies-for-beginners/) shows what to buy if you want to go further. [The Junk Journal Starter Bundle](/shop/#paper) gathers a book, adhesive and pen in one place.

### 10. A recipe-card night

**Feels like:** a little archive of your own, and a reason to eat well next week.
**Needs:** index cards or a notebook, a pen and the three recipes you actually cook.
**First step:** write the card for the dish you make without looking. Add the thing everybody asks about, the half-teaspoon you always put in. Copy two more, then tape in a grease-stained scrap from the old family recipe if you have one.

### 11. A little clay, over two evenings

**Feels like:** pressing your thumb into something, which turns out to be very satisfying.
**Needs:** a pack of air-dry clay and, on the second evening, paint. A drying night in between.
**First step:** pinch a ring dish tonight and let it dry as the pack says. Our [air-dry clay guide](/journal/air-dry-clay-for-beginners/) walks you through three small projects, and [the Air-Dry Clay Starter Bundle](/shop/#creative) has the clay and paint. If you would like to do it in company, some park and recreation departments list adult pottery classes, such as one Colorado city's, which says it offers daytime and evening sessions. Check your own department's class list for schedules.

### 12. Mahjong with three friends

**Feels like:** the evening you will talk about on the way home.
**Needs:** a set, a table and three people willing to be bad at it together.
**First step:** send the invitation first, before you feel ready. Our [mahjong night guide](/journal/how-to-host-a-mahjong-night/) covers the timeline, and [the Host Your First Mahjong Night bundle](/shop/#hosting) has the set and the card. Borrowing a set also works.

## Make it stick without making it a project

Choose one plan for this week, not three. Set it out in the morning (the mug, the notebook, the card with its stamp), and put the phone on charge in the hallway before you take your shoes off. The first try may feel a little flat, so give the same plan three different evenings before you decide it is not for you.

If you would rather paint than make something from scratch, [the Cozy Paint Night Bundle](/shop/#cozy) collects paint-by-numbers kits that turn an evening into a finished canvas.

## Questions

**What if I am too tired to do anything?** Use the 10-minute list, and treat the tea as the whole plan. Sitting still with nothing in your hands counts.

**What if I miss the phone?** Expect that. Use a plan that gives your hands something to do: sorting paper, chopping, stirring, writing.

**Do I need to buy anything?** No. Items 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 need what you already have. The shop bundles are there if an idea sticks and you want the proper tools.

**Can I do these with a partner or kids?** Most of them, yes. Tea, a walk, dinner, a recipe-card night and clay all work as shared plans.

## Your next step

Pick one plan from the shortest list. Tonight, put your phone on charge in another room, put the kettle on and sit by the window. The rest of the list will still be there when you want it.
