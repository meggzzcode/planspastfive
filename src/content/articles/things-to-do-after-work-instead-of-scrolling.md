You get home, put the keys in the bowl and sit down for one minute with your phone. Forty minutes later the light has changed, you could not say what you read, and dinner is still an idea. Nothing terrible happened. It just was not what you wanted from the evening.

Most lists of alternatives are lists of hobbies, and a hobby asks for a decision at the moment you have none left. This one sorts twelve small plans by **how much time you have**. Start with the chooser.

## Pick your evening in ten seconds

| What you have | On your own | With someone |
|---|---|---|
| 10 minutes, running on empty | Tea in the window seat (1) or a doorstep walk (2) | A postcard (3) or a voice note to a friend (4) |
| 30 minutes, some energy | A shelf tidied pretty (5) or a library hour (7) | A call with a time on it (6) or dinner, slower (8) |
| An evening, ready for plans | A class night (11), or a thrifting walk (9) at your own pace | Dinner with a friend (10), film night (12) or a class with a friend (11) |

## Making the switch

The phone wins because it is already in your hand. Beat it by making the better option just as close.

- **Put the phone out of reach before you sit.** Charge it in the hallway, not on the sofa arm. For bedtime, the NHS advises trying not to use phones, tablets or computers for an hour before bed.
- **Write an if-then plan.** "If I am home by 6, then I make tea and sit in the window seat." A US National Cancer Institute reference page on if-then plans (the page is archived) says they spell out when, where and how, and that vague plans offer little benefit.
- **Set it out and keep a default.** A mug on the counter, a card on the cushion, one plan you fall back on whenever you cannot choose.

## If you have 10 minutes

**1. Tea in the window seat.** Make it before you take your coat off, carry it to any seat with a view (a parking lot counts) and drink it with nothing else in your hands. Kitchen and kettle only? That is the whole plan.

**2. A walk from the doorstep.** Slip on your shoes, skip the route and the playlist, and go once around the block, turning left at every corner. Pick one thing you have walked past a hundred times and look at it properly. Works with a partner or a child, who can lead.

**3. A postcard to someone you miss.** Write three lines: where you saw something that made you think of them, and that you would like to see them soon. You need a card, a pen and a stamp, and the stamp is the one thing you may not own, so buy a few next time you are at the post office or grocery store. Leave it by the front door so it leaves with you tomorrow.

**4. A voice note to a friend.** Record sixty seconds about one good or funny thing from your day and send it. Nobody has to answer tonight. It is a call with the pressure taken off, and it works from a kitchen counter.

## If you have 30 minutes

**5. Tidy one shelf into a pretty one.** Take everything off, wipe it, and put back only what you like looking at. Lean one book, stand one candle, tuck in a postcard. Set a timer for 20 minutes and stop at one shelf.

**6. A call with a time on it.** Text in the morning: "Can I call you at 6:30? Twenty minutes, tops." A set time and a set length make it easy to say yes and easy to end. Fold laundry or stir a pan while you talk.

**7. A library hour.** Go in with no list. Library card rules vary, so check your library's website first. Browse one shelf you never visit, pick one book, and sit in one chair for twenty minutes before you leave. Ask at the desk whether your card also opens ebooks, which means the next book needs no trip.

**8. Dinner, slower.** Cook something you already know with a song on, and pick one step to do properly that you normally rush, such as dicing the onion evenly. Set the table with a real napkin and eat sitting down. Only the kitchen and the phone? Put the phone in the cupboard and the radio on.

## If you have a whole evening

**9. A thrifting walk with a limit.** Choose a thrift store or secondhand shop within walking distance, agree a small limit before you go, and browse one section properly: mugs, books or scarves. You are hunting for one small thing you would be glad to see every day, and buying nothing counts. Bring a tote bag so you do not need a new one, and take a friend if you can, since a second opinion makes browsing fun.

**10. Dinner with a friend, out or in.** Either book an early table within walking distance of one of you and agree on one course each, or make a soup you already cook and text "Come at 7, bring nothing". Early tables are easy to leave, and a pot on the stove lets guests eat at the counter. Send the message while the plan is still easy: "Thursday at 6?"

**11. A class night, drop-in or booked.** Many parks departments list adult classes. Toronto's says you can find a program near you and drop in at your convenience, and Westminster, Colorado's adult pottery page says daytime and evening classes are offered, with an online registration button. Search "[your town] parks and recreation adult classes," pick one for next week and put it in the calendar. Take a friend if you can.

**12. A film night with a plan.** Choose the film at lunch, put the snacks in a bowl and agree a start time (7:30, say) with whoever is home. With kids, let each pick in turn; with a partner, one of you chooses and the other makes the snacks. Lights low, no second screen.

## When the commute ate the evening

Use the 10-minute list first. Tea, the postcard or the voice note still fit, and a small thing done on purpose beats a big plan you skip. If you are home at 8, the call or the shelf is a fine choice, and so is a second cup of tea.

## If it does not work

- **Too many choices.** Choosing is hard by evening. Use the default (tea, window seat) and ignore the rest tonight.
- **You started too late.** Cut the plan in half. A five-minute doorstep walk still counts, and the whole thing can wait for tomorrow.
- **No plan, so the phone won.** Write the if-then plan on a sticky note now and keep it where the phone usually lands.
- **Not even ten minutes happened.** That happens, and one skipped night changes nothing. Put the phone on its charger, go to bed, and try the same plan tomorrow.
