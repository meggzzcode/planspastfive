You get home, put the keys in the bowl and sit down for one minute with your phone. Forty minutes later the light has changed, you could not say what you read, and dinner is still an idea. Nothing terrible happened. It just was not what you wanted from the evening.

Most lists of alternatives are lists of hobbies, and a hobby asks for a decision at the moment you have none left. This list sorts twelve small plans by **how much time you have**. Most need nothing you do not already own.

## Making the switch

Reaching for the phone takes no effort and no decision, so anything better has to be nearly as easy. Do the work before you sit down.

- **Move the phone, not your willpower.** Common Sense Media suggests keeping only everyday tools on your first screen and typing an app's name instead of tapping its icon, so there is a short pause before it opens. A charger in the hallway does the same job for the sofa.
- **Decide at lunch.** "Tonight is the doorstep walk" is a plan. "Something nicer than scrolling" is a mood.
- **Leave the next step in sight, and keep a default.** A mug on the counter, a card on the cushion, one plan you fall back on whenever you cannot choose.

You will still scroll some nights. A plan that survives an off night is the point.

## If you have 10 minutes

### 1. Tea in the window seat

Make it before you take your coat off, carry it to whichever seat has a view (a parking lot counts) and drink it looking out, with nothing else in your hands. When the mug is empty, you are done.

### 2. A walk from the doorstep

Wear shoes you can slip on, skip the route and the playlist, and go once around the block, turning left at every corner. Pick one thing you have walked past a hundred times, a doorway, a tree or a hand-painted sign, and really look at it.

### 3. A postcard to someone you miss

Write three lines: where you saw something that made you think of them, and that you would like to see them soon. You need a card or postcard, a pen and a stamp, and the stamp is the one thing you may not have, so pick up a few next time you are at the post office or the grocery store. Leave it by the front door so it goes out with you tomorrow.

### 4. A library card

Search your library's name plus "get a library card". Rules differ by system, but the ones we read asked adults for a photo ID and proof of address for a full card, and several offered an online card for ebooks and other digital resources. Boston Public Library, for example, says its eCard takes about five minutes to sign up for, with an in-person upgrade for physical items. Once you have one, reserve a single book so something is waiting for you.

## If you have 30 minutes

### 5. Tidy one shelf into a pretty one

Take everything off one shelf, wipe it down and put back only what you like looking at. Lean one book, stand one candle, tuck in a postcard. Stop at one shelf. The rest of the room can wait.

### 6. A call with a time on it

Text in the morning: "Can I call you at 6:30? Twenty minutes, tops." A set time and a set length make it easy to say yes and easy to end. Fold laundry or walk around the block while you talk, so your hands stay busy.

### 7. A bath with a plan

Decide the extras before you run the water: a paperback on a tray, a speaker playing something across the room, the phone in the next room. Only have a shower? Warm socks and ten pages of a book afterward give you the same shape.

### 8. Dinner, slower

Cook something you already know how to make, with a song or the radio on. Choose one step to do properly that you normally rush, such as dicing the onion evenly or toasting the spices, and set the table with a real napkin. Eat sitting down.

## If you have a whole evening

### 9. A thrifting walk with a limit

Choose a charity shop, a secondhand store or a flea market within walking distance and agree on a small limit before you go. Browse one section properly: the mugs, the books, the scarves. You are hunting for one small thing you would be glad to see every day. Going with a friend turns it into a social plan, and buying nothing counts as a good outcome.

### 10. Dinner with a friend, out or in

Either book an early table within walking distance of one of you and agree on one course each, or make a soup or stew you already cook and text "Come at 7, bring nothing". Early tables are easy to leave, and a pot on the stove lets guests eat at the counter. Send the message while the plan is still easy: "Thursday at 6?"

### 11. Three photos on display

Pick three photos you like, from a drawer, an old album or ones you print. Put them where you will see them every day: propped on a shelf, taped inside a cupboard door, leaned against the bathroom mirror. Choose the spot first and stop at three.

### 12. A film night with a plan

Choose the film at lunch, set the phone to charge in the hallway and decide the snack in advance. Watch it from the sofa with the lights low and no second screen. Make it feel like an outing: put the popcorn in a bowl and agree the start time (7:30, say) with whoever is home.

## When the commute has eaten the evening

Use the 10-minute list first. Tea in the window seat, the postcard or the library card still fit, and a small thing done on purpose beats a big plan you skip. If you are home at 8, the call or the bath is a fine choice, and so is a second cup of tea.

## When kids or a partner are home

Tea, the doorstep walk and the slower dinner all work as shared plans, and the dinner idea can include a partner or a kid if the place is relaxed or everyone has a job at home. For solo time, agree a handover ("I take the bath, you take bedtime") and put it on the calendar like an appointment.
