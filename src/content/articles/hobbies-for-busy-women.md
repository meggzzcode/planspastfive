It is 5:40 on a Thursday. The laptop is shut, your shoes are somewhere near the door, and you would like to make or do something that is entirely yours. You also have roughly the energy of a houseplant in January.

Most hobby lists start with what sounds interesting. This one starts with what you have left, because that is what decides whether a hobby happens or lives forever in a browser tab. Twelve ideas follow, grouped by energy, each with a first step small enough to do this week.

## Three questions, in this order

1. **How much have I got left?** Sofa only, one lamp and a table, or enough to go out or have people over.
2. **Do I want company?** Some hobbies are better with a friend, and some are better with nobody.
3. **What do I want to spend to find out?** Every hobby here has a gentle way in. Borrowing, secondhand and library cards all count as a fair start.

A note on money. The budget lines below are rough bands, not prices, and shops vary. Think of them as: **under a coffee-shop lunch**, **a small shopping trip** (one errand, a handful of items) or **needs a little investment** (worth borrowing or renting first).

## Nothing left: start in your work clothes

These need a surface the size of a tray and about 45 minutes. None of them asks you to make a decision you would call a decision.

**Reading**  
*30 minutes · solo · under a coffee-shop lunch (library)*

Put the shortest book you own where your phone usually lands, and put the phone on charge in another room. The first ten pages feel slow next to a screen. Give it ten, then decide.

**Junk journaling**  
*45 minutes · solo · under a coffee-shop lunch*

All you need is an old notebook, a glue stick and a drawer of paper scraps. Empty your bag and wallet, pick three bits from this week (a ticket, a receipt, a coffee sleeve) and make one page. It will look chaotic. That is the format. Our [first junk journal spread guide](/journal/your-first-junk-journal-spread/) has three layouts to copy.

**Embroidery**  
*45 minutes · solo · a small shopping trip*

Gather a hoop, fabric, thread and a needle. Learn one stitch, backstitch, and stitch your initial. Your first stitches will wobble, and a hoop in a tote bag counts as a plan for the train.

**Crochet**  
*45 minutes · solo · a small shopping trip*

Buy a hook and a skein of medium-weight yarn. The first hour is fiddly, so queue a beginner video before you sit down and aim for one granny square that does not have to be square.

## A little left: one table, one lamp

You can set something up and put it away again. Thirty to sixty minutes, one flat surface, solo or with a friend.

**Watercolor**  
*30 minutes · solo or a friend · a small shopping trip*

Get a small paint set, a brush or two and watercolor paper. Paint whatever is on your counter in three colours only. Fewer colours keeps it calm, and you can add a fourth if you miss it.

**Air-dry clay**  
*About an hour · solo or a friend · a small shopping trip*

Pick up a pack of clay and something to roll it with. Roll a ball, press your thumb in and pinch a ring dish. This is a two-evening hobby, because clay needs time to dry. Follow the instructions on the pack and paint it another night.

**Flower arranging**  
*30 minutes · solo or a friend · under a coffee-shop lunch*

One bunch of grocery-store flowers is the whole start. Split it into three small jars and trim the stems at an angle. Flowers are a repeat purchase, so decide whether a weekly bunch is a treat or a chore before it becomes a habit.

## Enough left to go somewhere, or have people over

These take an evening or an afternoon and, mostly, a second person. They are the ones that end up in the group chat.

**Craft and paint nights**  
*About 2.5 hours · friends · a small shopping trip, split*

Pick the craft before you invite anybody (bracelets are the classic) so the invitation names a plan. You are the host, so choose something that survives a crowded table and a glass of wine.

**Mahjong**  
*2 hours · friends · borrow a set; buying needs a little investment*

Borrow a set if anyone in your circle has one. Plan the first night as practice with the tiles face up. It has a real learning curve, and nobody minds once they know that going in.

**Picnicking**  
*2 hours · friend, or alone with a book · under a coffee-shop lunch to a small shopping trip*

Claim a patch of grass on the next dry day, bring a blanket and a bag you can carry in one hand, and name a backup evening for when the weather has other ideas. The cheese is where the budget creeps.

**Pickleball**  
*About an hour · with people · borrow first; a paddle needs a little investment*

Ask at the court or your local parks page whether open play is on and whether equipment can be borrowed. Courts and times vary by town, so check before you go, and arrive with a friend.

**Tennis**  
*About an hour · a friend · needs a little investment*

A borrowed or secondhand racket is a sensible way to find out whether you like it. Rally for thirty minutes with no score. This is the highest-effort option here, which makes it a Saturday pick more than a Thursday one.

## Two quick filters

**Solo is the whole point:** reading, junk journaling, embroidery, crochet, and any of the "a little left" three on your own.

**Better with other people:** craft and paint nights, mahjong, pickleball, tennis, and picnicking if you can talk someone into it.

If money is your main constraint, start with reading, junk journaling, flower arranging or a borrowed-gear picnic, and save the rest for when you know what you love. If you are ready to browse gear for the paper-and-craft side, the [Shop](/shop/) collects starter picks as we verify them.

## Pick the smallest first step

A hobby does not have to be impressive, finished or shareable. It has to be easy enough to begin on a night when you would rather do nothing, and pleasant enough that you want to begin again.

Choose three cards that made you think "maybe", and leave out the one you think you ought to like. Write them on a sticky note and stick it to the fridge. Then take the one with the smallest first step and do only that step on the next evening you have an hour. Lopsided results count.
