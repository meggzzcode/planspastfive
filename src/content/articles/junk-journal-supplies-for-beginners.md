Picture the page. A cream envelope tucked under a ticket stub, one strip of tape in a colour that makes you smile, a line of your own handwriting underneath. Turn it over and the next page is just as full. The corners have gone soft, and it looks like something loved for a year, although it has only been a month.

That is a junk journal, and it takes four things to begin: a sturdy book, something to stick with, a pen and a roll of tape. Pick a mood for inspiration, buy one small basket of essentials, and your first page feels like the start of a collection.

**The kit at a glance.** Essentials: a heavy-paper book, dry adhesive and a black pen, plus tape and scissors you probably own already. Optional: a washi assortment for colour and aged paper pieces for a finishing touch. See all of it together on [the paper shelf in the Shop](/shop/#paper).

## Choose your look

These three looks are inspiration, not three shopping lists. All of them start from the same basket of essentials below, so you can pick a mood now and change your mind later. A book that sticks to one palette looks designed, even when it is chaos underneath.

**Shoebox Post.** Warm and well travelled, like a postal archive. Kraft and cream papers, black ink, aged paper pieces.
- *Collect:* airmail envelopes, postmarks, boarding passes, the receipt from a very good lunch.

## The starter basket

Start with these supplies, whichever look you choose: a heavy-paper book, dry adhesive and a pigment-ink pen, plus tape and scissors from the drawer. Everything else is paper, and the pretty extras further down are optional.

### 1. A book that can take glue

Layered paper and adhesive make a page swell, and a thin writing notebook ripples like a crisp packet. You want heavy paper, a binding that lies open and, ideally, a hard cover. A little colour on the page helps too: you can add it with paper.

{{pick:canson-graduate-mixed-media-5-5x8-5}}

Fill it from the front and let the spine fatten slowly. One envelope and a line of ink on a heavy white page already look like the start of a spread.

### 2. Something to stick with

{{pick:tombow-mono-permanent}}

Run it along the back of each piece in one steady stroke, pressing as you go. Line the piece up first, because it will not lift.

### 3. One good black pen

{{pick:staedtler-pigment-liner-black-3}}

Three widths are plenty to start. If you want more tip sizes, {{product:staedtler-pigment-liner-black-8}} runs from very fine to broad.

Write your captions last, once everything is stuck down, so the words fit the gaps. Date every spread.

### 4. Tape that sets the colour

Any tape you already own will do to start, and a washi assortment adds colour:

{{pick:scotch-expressions-washi-whimsical-15}}

This assortment is a warm, busy mix of stripes, leaf prints and a copper foil roll: playful rather than romantic, so a few rolls will suit Shoebox Post best. If your book is more modern than vintage, {{product:scotch-expressions-washi-abstract-modern-8}} is the cleaner-looking choice. Press tape down from one end, smoothing as you go, and pick the pattern that answers the colour of your envelope or ticket. Look at the listing images first; no assortment suits every palette.

A small pair of scissors for trimming tape ends and cutting out pieces is the one tool you will want, and you almost certainly own a pair already.

## Paper worth saving

The raw material is usually already in the recycling: envelopes, ticket stubs, tea-bag wrappers, an old paperback page, wrapping paper offcuts, stamps. Keep it all in an envelope for a week and you will have a page's worth by Friday.

## Your first spread, in seven steps

Set a timer for 45 minutes and treat it as a warm-up.

1. **Open the book flat** and press the middle with your palm.
2. **Lay out before you stick.** Put five or six pieces down loosely: one large, two medium, three small.
3. **Overlap, do not float.** Let pieces tuck under or over one another so the page holds together.
4. **Stick the biggest first,** then work down in size, lining each piece up before it touches the page.
5. **Add one strip of tape** across a corner or an edge. One. Step back.
6. **Write something small:** a date and a sentence about where the ticket came from.
7. **Close the book** and leave it under a stack of cookbooks overnight.

## Questions beginners ask

**Do I need real vintage paper?** No. Modern receipts, packaging and printed napkins look wonderful.

**Can I use my own photos?** Yes. Use copies of anything irreplaceable. Makers describe some adhesives as photo-safe, but that is their statement, and a conservator is the right source for precious prints.

**What if I hate my page?** Cover it. A bigger piece of paper, a strip of tape and a new caption turn a page you dislike into a layer.
